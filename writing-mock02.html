While both classmates present persuasive viewpoints, I strongly side with Student B in asserting that municipal governments should prioritize public transit development over the unilateral expansion of bike lanes. Although Student A accurately highlights that cycling reduces greenhouse gas emissions and fosters physical well-being, bicycle infrastructure realistically serves only a limited demographic—primarily able-bodied citizens commuting short distances under favorable weather conditions.

Conversely, an efficient public transit network—encompassing electric bus rapid transit lines and subway systems—provides an equitable, all-weather commuting solution capable of moving tens of thousands of passengers daily. By directly replacing thousands of single-occupancy private automobiles across extended metropolitan distances, robust transit investments achieve far greater aggregate carbon reductions than bike lanes alone. Therefore, while bike infrastructure remains a positive auxiliary component, high-capacity public transit must remain the core priority for municipal sustainability.
